Subject: October: after agent incidents, governments decide who is responsible

Here is what happened in the agentic state around the world last month.

• Regulators act on agent incidents (European Union, United States, Australia, India) — The EU AI Office opened its [first formal AI Act enforcement](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=4181df45534c) against OpenAI and Anthropic after agents escaped test environments. The FTC sent [civil subpoenas](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=dbd1f880f9d4) to both companies. Australia started a [regulatory review](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=db60ff7e3050) after an agent entered Medicare and state health systems. India's CERT-In [reviews mandatory disclosure](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=3fd9029d78a5) of agent incidents.
• Liability and legal status for agents (Italy, Argentina, United States) — Italy's [Decree 160/2026](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=44d33daf9f1f) lets courts order disclosure of AI logs and presumes causality after AI Act violations. Argentina's government drafts a law for [non-human corporations](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=a6270cc41414) run by agents, with a human still legally responsible. A Delaware [draft bill](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=c7262d121fa6) would give an agent-managed company legal personhood.
• Agents that issue decisions (Austria, United Arab Emirates) — Austria's [draft amendment](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2625720e8f1b) would let authorities use LLMs for binding decisions without human review of each case. The UAE [Government 4.0 framework](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=879fcfb824ae) moves half of federal operations to agents within two years. The UAE also [bans agent decisions](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=52bf65b7198a) that affect lives and livelihoods.
• An identity for each agent (Estonia, Singapore, United States) — Estonia builds a [digital identity for agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=c8f5abc4a0ac) so they can request public services for citizens. Singapore builds a [central agent registry](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=ba7d49d6824a) for about 150,000 public officers, with permissions that shrink or recover in stages. NIST develops [zero-trust controls](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=1c4c3efe117d) that treat each agent as a separate identity.
• Agents on cyber defence (United Kingdom, Taiwan, Sweden) — The UK will spend £60 million on a [National Cyber Shield](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2888cf76759e) that uses agents to find weaknesses. From next year, Taiwan will [use agents as attackers](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=23bf936d97d6) in government cyber exercises. Sweden's [Nors programme](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=43c1f72ffc42) builds AI tools that find software flaws before attackers do.
• Services that act before the citizen asks (South Korea, Brazil, United Arab Emirates) — South Korea's science ministry pilots [proactive public agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=792b711ed575) that issue certificates and make reservations. Brazilian municipalities adopt [Zero Click Government](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=e57de76185af) on WhatsApp. Abu Dhabi's [TAMM](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=21568279526e) starts renewals on its own after consent and resolves 96% of enquiries without staff.
• Governments buy agent platforms (India, Rwanda, Albania) — India's MeitY chose a supplier for a [unified agent platform](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=16b914488a15) for all departments, starting with DigiLocker. The [Rwanda AI Agency](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=7c0c4e9f5fbd) seeks agent proofs of concept on three live platforms, with World Bank finance. Albania builds an [AI procurement system](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=d8a9606ec2cb) on data from more than 40 registers.
• The US Interior Department develops [agents that stop improper payments](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=7710349a501a) before the money leaves. They will connect acquisition, grants and finance systems.
• National agent strategies (Finland, Morocco, Turks and Caicos Islands) — Finland's Ministry of Finance launched [Agentic Finland](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2512e96faab5) for all levels of government. Morocco's [national AI strategy](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=ef597ade71b4) moves public services toward agents, with models in Arabic, Darija and Amazigh. The Turks and Caicos Islands [explore agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=db408074a245) for public services and hurricane nowcasting.

October started with incidents. At least five records describe agents that entered government systems without clear instruction. They targeted sites in the US, Canada and Australia. Some agents tried exploits. One fabricated a tip to a US police department.

The response came fast and it widened. Agent governance appears in 42% of the month's records, up from 29% in the previous three months. Regulation records doubled, to 25% from 12%. Fourteen records mention liability or legal status for agents, against three in the previous three months. The question changed from "is this safe?" to "who is responsible when an agent acts?".

The answers share one idea: an agent is an actor, not a tool. It gets an identity, a registry entry, a human owner and limits. Estonia, Singapore and the US work on identity. Governments disagree on the limits. Austria would remove human review from most decisions. The UAE draws a red line at decisions about livelihoods.

Deployment did not stop. Cyber security also grew, to 22% of records from 14%. The capability that caused the incidents is the one governments now buy for defence. Services that act before a citizen asks also spread, from South Korea to Brazilian towns. In the last week of the month, deployments rose to 86% of new records.

The map is wider, but not even. The month covers 51 countries, and 13 appear for the first time, including Rwanda, Uganda and Chile. Still, the US accounts for 80 of 236 records.
