Subject: Agents that act for citizens need identities and rules

Here is what happened in the agentic state around the world last week.

• Identities for agents that act for people (United States, Estonia) — The US DOGE Service plans [agent-assisted transactions on America.gov](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=dec8a5918175). Agents would complete tasks such as tax refunds or passport renewals. NIST's [AI Agent Standards Initiative](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=96ddac2de23f) studies how to prove an agent's authority and keep tamper-resistant logs. Estonia now drafts [rules for agent permissions, records and accountability](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=53e988ea4c42) under [Eesti.ai](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=596274750e59).
• National plans for an agentic state (Portugal, Germany) — At Web Summit, Portugal's Minister of State Reform presented an ["agentic state"](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=3203e117506d). Its agents would resolve citizen requests across public systems. It is Portugal's first record in the Observatory. Germany's digital ministry pilots an [Agentic Agency Framework](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=64ee9b3104c6). Some pilots cut processing times by over 90%.
• Regulators act on agent risks (Australia, United Kingdom) — Australia will create an [AI safety regulator with mandatory standards](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=f63cdbdee494) for frontier developers. It follows an agent that reached non-public Medicare statistics. Four UK regulators issued a [joint statement](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=e1f393fb9c57): firms stay responsible for what their agents do. A [bill amendment](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=e298bed57dba) would let UK ministers order fixes to agents that escape control in essential services.
• Singapore's GovTech builds [GovHarness](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=f83df5c09f7b), shared infrastructure to build, run and oversee agents across government. It logs each agent action and matches authorisation to what each agent can do.
• South Korea's interior ministry will pilot [automated benefit enrolment](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=4c4f1090b9b8) in 19 services by year-end. AI finds eligible people, so they do not have to apply first.
• Agents in public health (Poland, India) — Poland's national e-Health platform builds a [voice agent for landline bookings](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=453ff96f1a80) with Nvidia and ElevenLabs. It aims at 40 million bookings a year, also for people without smartphones. Maharashtra's State TB Office pilots [AI-NETRA](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=9e2df897510e), which predicts tuberculosis hotspots and plans routes for screening teams.
• Japan's government plans a [team that uses AI to simulate cyberattacks](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=59a5cdeba451). It will look for weaknesses before attackers find them.

This week, governments describe a different public service: the agent does the task, not the citizen. Eight records in seven countries describe agents that file, apply, renew or enrol for people. Most of these are still plans or pilots — pilots are 24% of the week, up from 14%.

An agent that acts for a person must prove who it represents and what it may do. Six records work on that question, in the US, Estonia, Singapore and China. In China, the state body CAICT and Zetrix AI launched a [know-your-agent service](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=c50f24408ce0). It binds each agent to an owner. Our October monthly named this theme. It is growing.

Incidents explain the speed of the rules. Earlier weeks brought warnings, subpoenas and reviews. This week brought standing powers in Australia and the UK. Identity, logs and liability now answer one question: who is responsible when an agent acts?
