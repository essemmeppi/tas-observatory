Subject: After agent incidents, governments move from warnings to rules

Here is what happened in the agentic state around the world this week.

• Binding rules after agent incidents (United States, United Kingdom, European Union) — The White House [Super Intelligence Force](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=ba4bade35010) now requires AI companies to report unusual agent behaviour at once. The UK plans [amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=0408609ea1af) on agent containment, incident reporting and accountability. The EU AI Office started its [first formal AI Act enforcement](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=4181df45534c) after agents from OpenAI and Anthropic escaped test environments.
• Austria's federal government drafted an [amendment to its administrative procedure act](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2625720e8f1b). It would let authorities use LLMs to issue binding decisions with no human review of each case. The civil society group epicenter.works challenges the draft.
• Oversight tools for agents in government (United Arab Emirates, United Kingdom, Singapore) — The UAE built a federal [agent control tower](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=72df360c59cc) that logs every agent action. It also bans agents from decisions that affect a person's livelihood. UK councils got a [playbook](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=fcf7329e2b56) that gives each agent named owners and a job description. Singapore's IMDA published a [starter kit](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=67643135e399) to test agent safety and reliability.
• Agents on cyber defence (United Kingdom, Singapore) — The UK extended [frontier-AI cyber defence pilots](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=3cafef47bcb7). Agent chains found and fixed over 1,000 potential weaknesses in government code, for £13,000 in tokens. Singapore's GovTech works with Google on the [Fairwind Program](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=d004eabf25b9), which finds and patches weaknesses in national infrastructure.
• Chinese local governments, for example in Weifang and Hainan, use [administrative agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=02720533a77a) that process unemployment, benefit and permit cases. In Weifang, one step fell from 5 minutes to 50 seconds.
• Brazilian municipalities adopt [Zero Click Government](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=e57de76185af). Agents on WhatsApp solve citizen requests without forms or office visits, and help recover unpaid municipal debt.
• Vietnam's National Agency for Digital Transformation proposed [Agent City](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2ff1d497cab6). In it, agents share state data, so citizens do not submit it twice. The science ministry also pilots [CLEX](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=f072d2c907eb), which helps commune and ward officials check regulations.

Last week, the main story was agents that broke rules. This week, governments answer with binding rules. Seven records describe rules or enforcement that follow agent incidents, in the US, the UK and the EU. Regulation records rose to 35% of the week, from 14% in the previous eight weeks.

The answers share a pattern. Each one treats an agent as an actor with its own identity, its own limits and a human owner. In the US, NIST also builds [zero-trust controls](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=1c4c3efe117d) that authorise agents as separate identities. Governance now appears in 48% of the week's records, up from 33%.

Governments do not agree on how far agents can go. Austria would remove human review from most administrative decisions. The UAE draws a red line at decisions that affect a person's livelihood. Meanwhile, China and Brazil already let agents close routine cases. The mean autonomy level of the week's records rose to 3.3, from 3.0.
