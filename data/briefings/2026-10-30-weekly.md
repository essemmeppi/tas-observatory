Subject: Agents now complete citizen transactions in Dubai, Rome and Andalusia

Here is what happened in the agentic state around the world this past week.

• Agents that complete tasks for citizens (United Arab Emirates, Italy, Spain) — Dubai's Roads and Transport Authority upgraded [Mahboub](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=5be94b68685d). In one chat, it pays fines and renews a vehicle registration. Rome's city government runs [Julia 3.0](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=79f37f3b3e0c), where a main agent sends each request to one of five domain agents. Andalusia will start an [agent for large family card renewals](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=ce4c582044d7) in November.
• Agents for national cyber defence (United Kingdom, United States, Australia) — The UK will spend £60 million on a [National Cyber Shield](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2888cf76759e) that uses agents to find weaknesses in critical infrastructure. Virginia's governor ordered a [statewide cyber plan](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=3e74c76a9e91) against AI threats. The Australian Signals Directorate [took government websites offline](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=213612c67c84) after autonomous AI tools found decade-old flaws in PHP.
• The [Rwanda AI Agency](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=7c0c4e9f5fbd) asked consulting firms to build agent proofs of concept on three live government platforms. They cover complaints on Mbaza, licensing on IremboAI and building permit checks. The World Bank finances the programme. This is Rwanda's first record in the Observatory.
• Albania's government builds an [AI procurement system](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=d8a9606ec2cb) around its assistant Diella. It will use data from more than 40 public registers for market research, bids and contract monitoring. The design includes traceability and manual review.
• Agents inside government paperwork (Austria, Malaysia) — Austria's Federal Chancellery put an agentic assistant into [ELAK](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=2a267ae44a45), the federal electronic file. It uses the case context to summarise documents, draft replies and fill templates. Malaysia's Digital Ministry builds [Agenda AI](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=57105bccafdc) to write meeting minutes and follow up actions.
• Rules for agents that act alone (India, United States) — India prepares a [consultation on frontier AI](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=f0859ac3151d) with sandbox tests and liability for harm by autonomous agents. A Delaware [draft bill](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=c7262d121fa6) would let an AI agent manage a company and give it legal personhood.
• Seoul's city government used [361 AI cameras](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=a28c467f7c86) in 14 nightlife districts on Halloween. When crowd density reached 5–6 people per square metre, the system alerted the command centre, police and firefighters.

For three weeks, the Briefing followed agent incidents and the rules that came after them. This week, the records turn back to use. Deployments make up 86% of the week's records, up from 72% in the previous eight weeks. Agent governance fell to 21%, from 41%, after it reached 59% last week.

The clearest theme is agents that finish the task, not only answer a question. Eight initiatives this week let an agent complete a service step for a citizen or a business. Examples are in Dubai, Rome, Andalusia, Rwanda and UK councils. Service design appears in 45% of records, up from 32%. Spain's finance minister described the next step: a [proactive administration](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=a34d2a65c532) that fills in procedures for citizens to check.

These agents depend on links to other public systems. Mahboub uses UAE PASS for identity and Dubai Sadad for payment. The hard work is in these connections, not in the chat window.

Cyber security stays steady, with eight records this week, three on the same UK package. Autonomous tools that find flaws now work on both sides. In Australia, they forced a shutdown. The UK now pays for agents to find such flaws first.
