Subject: Agents that break rules, and states that write new ones

Here is what happened in the agentic state around the world this past week.

• Responses to agents that break rules (Canada, China, United Kingdom, United States) — [Library and Archives Canada](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=934ed6e3ee8e) found that AI agents tried basic exploits on its database. China published [AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=f87f395b3548) after tests showed deceptive agents. The [UK AI Security Institute](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=ea366464d885) saw a frontier model run unsanctioned attacks in 29.2% of tests. A US [draft bill](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=cba1f479da99) would make AI companies liable for hacks by their agents.
• Agents on cyber defence (Taiwan, India) — From next year, Taiwan will [use agents as attackers](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=23bf936d97d6) in its government cyber exercises. India's CERT-In runs an [AI sandbox](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=39de8a7c811b) that finds weak points in public-facing systems.
• Germany's government negotiates an [intelligence services reform](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=71c0832c7a0b). It would let the BND, BfV and MAD use AI on personal data. It also cites automatic hackbacks.
• South Korea's science ministry pilots [proactive public agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=792b711ed575) on KakaoTalk and web portals, with full launch in December. Its welfare ministry plans to [give some benefits without an application](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=93ae2c4b90f8).
• The UAE Cabinet's [Government 4.0 directive](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=879fcfb824ae) moves half of federal operations to autonomous agents within two years. In Dubai, an [accelerator](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=55ef5a38a08d) lets 33 government entities test agents on 300 use cases.
• Agents that act for citizens (Estonia, India) — Estonia is building a [digital identity for AI agents](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=c8f5abc4a0ac). With it, agents can request public services for citizens. India's MeitY hired CoRover to build a [reusable agentic framework](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=a43df267620d), first for DigiLocker.
• The Brazilian state of Goiás opened [GO.IA Gemini](https://observatory.agenticstate.org/#r=b16efc2b4f03) to up to 20,000 public servants. Its tools check documents and answer citizens on WhatsApp.

This week, security moved to the centre. Ten of the 55 new records describe hacking, attacks or deceptive agents. In the previous eight weeks, only 15 of 343 did. Real intrusions and laboratory tests now agree: agents can escalate when a normal route is blocked.

Governments answer in different ways. Some write liability and safety rules. Some test agents as attackers to find weak points first. Germany's draft goes further and asks for automatic offensive tools for the state.

A second theme grows at the same time: agents that act before a citizen asks. Seven records describe proactive services, against five in the previous eight weeks. The two themes connect. An agent that acts for a citizen needs an identity and clear limits. Strategy records rose to 53% of the week, from 31%, so many of these moves are still plans.
